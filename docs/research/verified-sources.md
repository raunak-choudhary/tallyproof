## Notes per item

### 1. FINRA
- Primary pages: overview https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/reports/2026-finra-annual-regulatory-oversight-report (dated December 09, 2025), GenAI section URL in table.
- "Must test for and govern" overstates it. The report lists "effective practices" and "considerations", and its introduction says it "does not create any new legal or regulatory requirements or new interpretations of existing requirements." Say "names" and "lists as effective practices", not "requires".
- Useful extra quotes for the thesis: "validation and human-in-the-loop review of model outputs, including performing regular checks for errors or bias", and "storing prompt and output logs for accountability and troubleshooting". The section also covers AI agents for the first time.

### 2. EU AI Act and Digital Omnibus
- Original Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 Article 113 had most high-risk obligations applying from 2 August 2026. That is no longer the law.
- Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI): Parliament plenary 16 June 2026 (423 for, 57 against, 174 abstentions), Council adoption 29 June 2026, signed 8 July 2026, published in the OJ 24 July 2026. Parliament's text states its position "corresponds to the final legislative act, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744".
- New dates: 2 December 2027 (stand-alone, Annex III) and 2 August 2028 (embedded in products, Annex I). Article 50(2) machine-readable marking gets a grace period to 2 December 2026 for systems on the market before 2 August 2026.
- Caveat on access: EUR-Lex and consilium.europa.eu returned bot-challenge or 403 responses to my fetches, so the dates above are quoted from the European Parliament Legislative Train page and the Commission press release IP/26/1024, both official EU sources. A corrigendum dated 29 September 2026 is reported by a third-party standards index; I could not open it. Link the EUR-Lex ELI anyway as the canonical text.
- Relevance caveat: a streaming financial ledger with an AI analyst is not automatically "high-risk" under Annex III. Credit scoring of natural persons is; generic financial analytics is not. Say Articles 12 and 14 describe the logging and oversight properties regulators expect for high-risk AI, not that the project is subject to them.

### 3. SR 26-2
- It exists. The brief's description is wrong in two ways: (a) the text does not mention an "audit trail"; (b) footnote 3 explicitly excludes generative and agentic AI from scope, so it cannot be cited as expecting audit trails for an LLM analyst.
- Text was extracted from SR2602a1.pdf. The documentation section says documentation supports "the tracking of recommendations, responses, and exceptions". The footnote does add that a bank's "risk management and governance practices should guide the determination of appropriate governance and controls for any tools, processes, or systems not covered in this document."
- Parallel issuances (OCC Bulletin 2026-13, FDIC FIL-15-2026) are reported by secondary sources only; not verified here.
- Recommendation: drop it, or use the honest wording in the table.

### 4. AWS QLDB
- Both AWS URLs now 301-redirect (the blog to aws.amazon.com/products/databases/, the docs page returns 404, aws.amazon.com/qldb/ redirects to Aurora). Quotes were taken from Wayback Machine snapshots of the AWS pages. Cite the original AWS URL plus the archive link.
- Companion post: "Migrate an Amazon QLDB Ledger to Amazon Aurora PostgreSQL" (Dan Blaner, 18 Jul 2024), archived at http://web.archive.org/web/20250618043739/https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/database/migrate-an-amazon-qldb-ledger-to-amazon-aurora-postgresql/
- "Retired" is fine colloquially; AWS's own wording is "end of support".

### 5 to 12. Research
- 5: Use the ACM DOI as the venue citation and arXiv for open access. Do not write "57% of citations lacked faithfulness" as a general rate; it is a worst case ("up to").
- 7: The brief implied this was a finding about RAG systems generally. It is one frontier model in one shared task, scored by an LLM judge (RAGAs). Their own corrective pipeline only "marginally improved citation faithfulness". Fine to cite with the exact framing in the table, or drop.
- 8: The 81% is for one configuration (GPT-4-Turbo with a shared vector store) on 150 questions; date it (2023). Models have improved since.
- 9: The 42.3 is post-hoc generation on the dev set; quote the setting.
- 10: Title in brief is wrong. Low authority; RFC 9162 and Crosby and Wallach are stronger evidence for Merkle proof performance and size.
- 12: Honest README summary: answerproof is a small MIT Python library (v0.1.1, created July 2026, no stars at check time) that builds a signed JSON receipt per RAG answer containing query, answer, principal and permissions, model and params, a SHA-256 hash per retrieved source, a Merkle root over source hashes, n-gram-overlap citations and a rule-based grounding score, signed with Ed25519 over a canonical payload. Verification checks signature, Merkle root, source hashes, grounding and optional signer pin. Its own threat model says it does not prove truth, does not prove semantic correctness of grounding ("Citation binding is n-gram overlap"), does not manage key trust, and does not prove retrieval was complete. Difference from this project: answerproof commits to the retrieval set per answer; it is not an append-only log with consistency proofs over a growing event stream.
